    Methodology

    Each street-level incident published by West Midlands Police is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every ward we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its centre, and normalise against Birmingham's own rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are the 69 Birmingham City Council wards from the ONS ward set. The scale is calibrated to Birmingham and cannot be read against another city's number — a Birmingham 55 and a London 55 both mean “typical for this city”, not the same amount of crime. data.police.uk publishes one calendar month at a time, so these pages describe a single month rather than a year, and a typical Birmingham ward carries fewer incidents than a typical inner-London one — which means figures here move more from month to month, and small differences between neighbouring wards are not meaningful. Three ward centres (Sutton Vesey, Sutton Walmley & Minworth, Edgbaston) sit in large parks or green belt and have been moved to the built-up part of the same ward, because a circle drawn over parkland reads as safe when it is really empty. Time-of-day charts use the category mix, as data.police.uk does not publish incident times. Pages regenerate as new data is published.

    The index compares areas within Birmingham. It is not comparable between cities: each police force publishes a different set of offences over a different period — Birmingham's figures cannot be read against another city's on the same 0–100 scale.
