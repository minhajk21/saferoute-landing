Is Toronto safe at night?

53% of Toronto's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 22% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 19% to 79% depending on which neighbourhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether Toronto is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is Toronto safe to walk at night?

53% of Toronto's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 22% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighbourhoods than it does between cities, from 19% to 79% here. The useful question is which neighbourhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighbourhood in Toronto has the most reported crime at night?

Downtown Yonge East, with roughly 830 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 48% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighbourhood changes most after dark?

Edenbridge-Humber Valley is the most night-concentrated in Toronto: 79% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a Toronto median of 54%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighbourhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

How does SafeRoute use this at night?

The app scores each walking route against the same Toronto Police data, and can weight it toward the time you are actually walking rather than a flat all-day average. It also shows how much of each route runs on lit streets, and lets you share a walk and check in on arrival.

Methodology

Every incident reported to the Toronto Police Service carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighbourhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighbourhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each incident reported to the Toronto Police Service (Major Crime Indicators — assault, robbery, break & enter, auto theft and theft over, via the TPS Public Safety Data Portal) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighbourhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its centre, and normalize against citywide crime rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are the City of Toronto's 158 official neighbourhoods, grouped under the six former municipalities (Old Toronto, North York, Scarborough, Etobicoke, East York and York) that people still use as districts. Time-of-day charts use TPS occurrence timestamps, severity-weighted. Pages regenerate as new data is published.
