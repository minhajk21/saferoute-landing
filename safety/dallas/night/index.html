Is Dallas safe at night?

55% of Dallas's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 22% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 23% to 76% depending on which neighborhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether Dallas is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is Dallas safe to walk at night?

55% of Dallas's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 22% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighborhoods than it does between cities, from 23% to 76% here. The useful question is which neighborhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighborhood in Dallas has the most reported crime at night?

Downtown, with roughly 2,988 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 56% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighborhood changes most after dark?

Deep Ellum is the most night-concentrated in Dallas: 76% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a Dallas median of 53%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighborhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

Methodology

Every incident reported to the Dallas Police Department carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighborhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighborhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each incident reported to the Dallas Police Department (via Dallas Open Data) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center over the last twelve months, and normalize against Dallas's own rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. The scale is calibrated to Dallas and cannot be read against another city's number. Boundaries are the City of Dallas GIS registry of neighborhood associations, cleaned to place names — "Deep Ellum Community Association" is published as Deep Ellum. That registry covers the parts of Dallas that organized, not every square mile, so this is a guide to the neighborhoods it names rather than a map of the whole city, and a few pages describe overlapping ground. Where a published center sat in parkland or a creek corridor rather than among houses, it has been moved to the deepest built-up point inside the same boundary. Time-of-day charts use Dallas PD occurrence times, severity-weighted. Pages regenerate as new data is published.
