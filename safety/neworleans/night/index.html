Is New Orleans safe at night?

48% of New Orleans's crime-related 911 calls happen between 6pm and 6am, and 18% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 27% to 56% depending on which neighborhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether New Orleans is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is New Orleans safe to walk at night?

48% of New Orleans's crime-related 911 calls happen between 6pm and 6am, and 18% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighborhoods than it does between cities, from 27% to 56% here. The useful question is which neighborhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighborhood in New Orleans has the most reported crime at night?

Iberville, with roughly 2,036 crime-related 911 calls between 6pm and 6am — 53% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighborhood changes most after dark?

City Park is the most night-concentrated in New Orleans: 56% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a New Orleans median of 45%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighborhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

How does SafeRoute use this at night?

The app scores each walking route against the same NOPD dispatch data, and can weight it toward the time you are actually walking rather than a flat all-day average. It also shows how much of each route runs on lit streets, and lets you share a walk.

Methodology

Every incident dispatched to the NOPD carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighborhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighborhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

New Orleans is the one city here not scored on filed police reports. NOPD's incident-report series carries no coordinates and no current-year data, so the only fresh geocoded feed is the Orleans Parish Communication District's 911 dispatch log. That log is mostly NOT crime — its largest call types are area checks, alarms, medical calls and traffic — so we keep only genuine offense call types (battery, robbery, burglary, theft, auto theft, weapons, drugs and similar) and discard the rest. Each surviving call is weighted by severity, summed within 1 km of a neighborhood's center, and normalized against citywide rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. A dispatch means police were sent, not that an offense was confirmed, and this under-counts against a true report feed — so New Orleans scores are comparable within the city but should not be read against another city's number. Boundaries are the city's 72 Neighborhood Statistical Areas, less the U.S. Naval Base and Lake Catherine — the latter because the dispatch feed records nothing anywhere inside it, and a page reading “0 incidents, 100/100” would present missing data as proven safety. Time-of-day charts use the real dispatch clock time. Pages regenerate as new data is published.

About this data. Figures are crime-related 911 calls police were dispatched to within 1 km of each neighborhood’s center — a dispatch means officers were sent, not that an offense was confirmed, and not every crime generates a call. This is informational only and not a guarantee of safety, a prediction, or a judgment of any community. Use it the way the app does: to pick better-lit, lower-incident routes and times.
