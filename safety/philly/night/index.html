Is Philadelphia safe at night?

38% of Philadelphia's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 12% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 14% to 52% depending on which neighborhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether Philadelphia is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is Philadelphia safe to walk at night?

38% of Philadelphia's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 12% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighborhoods than it does between cities, from 14% to 52% here. The useful question is which neighborhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighborhood in Philadelphia has the most reported crime at night?

North Central, with roughly 2,050 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 51% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighborhood changes most after dark?

Stadium District is the most night-concentrated in Philadelphia: 52% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a Philadelphia median of 37%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighborhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

Methodology

Every incident reported to the Philadelphia Police carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighborhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighborhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each incident reported to the Philadelphia Police Department (via OpenDataPhilly) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center, and normalize against citywide crime rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are the 158 neighborhoods the city publishes, grouped by the 18 Planning Districts the City Planning Commission uses; seven are not scored because nobody lives in them — both halves of Fairmount Park, Wissahickon and Pennypack Parks, the two airports, and the Southwest refinery belt labelled "Industrial" — where near-zero reported crime would read as falsely "safe" rather than as empty land. Time-of-day charts use PPD dispatch timestamps, severity-weighted. Locations are published at block level for privacy. Pages regenerate as new data is published.
