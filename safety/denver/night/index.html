Is Denver safe at night?

40% of Denver's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 21% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 19% to 53% depending on which neighborhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether Denver is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is Denver safe to walk at night?

40% of Denver's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 21% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighborhoods than it does between cities, from 19% to 53% here. The useful question is which neighborhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighborhood in Denver has the most reported crime at night?

CBD, with roughly 1,766 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 40% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighborhood changes most after dark?

Belcaro is the most night-concentrated in Denver: 53% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a Denver median of 40%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighborhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

How does SafeRoute use this at night?

Methodology

Every incident reported to the Denver Police carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighborhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighborhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each offense reported to the Denver Police Department (via Denver Open Data) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted offenses within 1 km of its center, and normalize against citywide crime rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are the City and County of Denver's official statistical neighborhoods. Denver's neighborhoods are relatively large, so 1 km circles overlap less than in denser cities and adjacent areas separate more cleanly. Time-of-day charts use DPD offense timestamps, severity-weighted. Pages regenerate as new data is published.
