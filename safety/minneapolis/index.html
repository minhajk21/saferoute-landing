    Methodology

    Each incident reported to the Minneapolis Police Department (via Minneapolis Open Data) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center, and normalize against citywide rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are the city's own 87 neighborhoods, grouped by its own 11 communities. Minneapolis wraps its neighborhoods around real lakes, so a centre that would otherwise land on open water is moved to the built-up part of the same neighborhood — never onto a neighbouring one. Time-of-day charts use MPD incident timestamps, severity-weighted. Pages regenerate as new data is published.

    The index compares areas within Minneapolis. It is not comparable between cities: each police force publishes a different set of offences over a different period — Minneapolis's figures cannot be read against another city's on the same 0–100 scale.

    Sources. Crime data: Minneapolis Police Department incidents via Minneapolis Open Data, covering the 8 months to August 2026.     About this data. Figures are incidents reported to police within 1 km of each neighborhood's center — reporting practices vary and not all crime is reported. This is informational only and not a guarantee of safety, a prediction, or a judgment of any community. Use it the way the app does: to pick better-lit, lower-incident routes and times.
