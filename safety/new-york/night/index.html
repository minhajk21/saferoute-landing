Is New York safe at night?

47% of New York's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 17% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 29% to 62% depending on which neighborhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether New York is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is New York safe to walk at night?

47% of New York's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 17% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighborhoods than it does between cities, from 29% to 62% here. The useful question is which neighborhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighborhood in New York has the most reported crime at night?

Midtown-Times Square, with roughly 3,587 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 48% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighborhood changes most after dark?

Sunset Park (West) is the most night-concentrated in New York: 62% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a New York median of 48%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighborhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

Methodology

Every incident reported to the NYPD carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighborhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighborhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each incident reported to the NYPD (via NYC Open Data) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center (2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Area centroids), and normalize against citywide crime rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Time-of-day charts use NYPD incident timestamps, severity-weighted. Pages regenerate as new data is published.

Sources. Crime data: NYPD complaint data via NYC Open Data, covering the 6 months to June 2026. Neighborhood boundaries: NYC 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Areas. Basemap (streets, parks, shoreline): NYC Open Data. Analysis © SafeRoute.

About this data. Figures are incidents reported to police within 1 km of each neighborhood's center — reporting practices vary and not all crime is reported. This is informational only and not a guarantee of safety, a prediction, or a judgment of any community. Use it the way the app does: to pick better-lit, lower-incident routes and times.
