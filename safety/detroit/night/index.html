Is Detroit safe at night?

55% of Detroit's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 22% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 45% to 65% depending on which neighborhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether Detroit is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is Detroit safe to walk at night?

55% of Detroit's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 22% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighborhoods than it does between cities, from 45% to 65% here. The useful question is which neighborhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighborhood in Detroit has the most reported crime at night?

Downtown, with roughly 1,490 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 64% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighborhood changes most after dark?

Delray is the most night-concentrated in Detroit: 65% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a Detroit median of 54%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighborhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

Methodology

Every incident reported to the DPD carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighborhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighborhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each incident reported to the Detroit Police Department (RMS crime incidents, via City of Detroit Open Data) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center, and normalize against citywide rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are the city's own 205 official neighborhoods, grouped by the 7 City Council districts published in the same layer. Four are not scored because nobody lives in them — Belle Isle, the state park island in the river, and the Conner Creek, Russell and West Side industrial belts — where near-zero reported crime would read as falsely "safe" rather than as empty land. Detroit's neighborhoods are small and closely spaced, so the 1 km circles around adjacent centers overlap and neighbouring areas will show similar figures. Time-of-day charts use DPD incident timestamps, severity-weighted. Pages regenerate as new data is published.
