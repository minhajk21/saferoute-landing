Is Boston safe at night?

45% of Boston's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 17% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 39% to 60% depending on which neighborhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether Boston is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is Boston safe to walk at night?

45% of Boston's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 17% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighborhoods than it does between cities, from 39% to 60% here. The useful question is which neighborhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighborhood in Boston has the most reported crime at night?

Downtown, with roughly 915 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 50% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighborhood changes most after dark?

South Boston Waterfront is the most night-concentrated in Boston: 60% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a Boston median of 46%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighborhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

Methodology

Every incident reported to the Boston Police carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighborhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighborhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each incident reported to the Boston Police Department (via Analyze Boston) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center, and normalize against citywide crime rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are the BPDA's official neighborhood set; the Harbor Islands are excluded because the uninhabited park has no reported crime and would read as falsely "safe". Because Boston is compact and densely built, neighborhood centers sit close together and their 1 km circles overlap — adjacent areas will show similar figures. Time-of-day charts use BPD incident timestamps, severity-weighted. Pages regenerate as new data is published.
