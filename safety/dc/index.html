How safe is your DC neighborhood?

SafeRoute scores every Washington, D.C. neighborhood 0–100 from incidents reported to the DC Metropolitan Police — severity-weighted, within 1 km of each area's center, normalized citywide. Higher is safer. The same data powers the SafeRoute app's crime-aware walking routes.

    Methodology

    Each incident reported to the DC Metropolitan Police (via Open Data DC) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center, and normalize against citywide crime rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are DCGIS's 132 named neighborhoods, grouped by the 8 city wards. Time-of-day charts use MPD report timestamps, severity-weighted. Locations are published at block level for privacy. Pages regenerate as new data is published.

    The index compares areas within Washington, D.C.. It is not comparable between cities: each police force publishes a different set of offences over a different period — Washington, D.C.'s figures cannot be read against another city's on the same 0–100 scale.

    Sources. Crime data: DC Metropolitan Police crime incidents via Open Data DC, covering the 7 months to August 2026. Neighborhood and ward boundaries: DCGIS Open Data (132 named neighborhoods, 8 wards). Basemap © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). Analysis © SafeRoute.

    About this data. Figures are incidents reported to police within 1 km of each neighborhood's center — reporting practices vary and not all crime is reported. This is informational only and not a guarantee of safety, a prediction, or a judgment of any community. Use it the way the app does: to pick better-lit, lower-incident routes and times.
