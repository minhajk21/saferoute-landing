Is Baltimore safe at night?

52% of Baltimore's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 21% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 43% to 65% depending on which area you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether Baltimore is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is Baltimore safe to walk at night?

52% of Baltimore's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 21% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between areas than it does between cities, from 43% to 65% here. The useful question is which area and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which area in Baltimore has the most reported crime at night?

Downtown/Seton Hill, with roughly 1,327 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 52% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which area changes most after dark?

Inner Harbor/Federal Hill is the most night-concentrated in Baltimore: 65% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a Baltimore median of 51%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet area whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

How does SafeRoute use this at night?

The app scores each walking route against the same Baltimore Police data, and can weight it toward the time you are actually walking rather than a flat all-day average. It also shows how much of each route runs on lit streets, and lets you share a walk and check in on arrival.

Methodology

Every incident reported to the Baltimore Police carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A area's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each area by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each incident reported to the Baltimore Police Department (via Open Baltimore) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every area we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center, and normalize against citywide crime rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are BNIA-JFI's 56 Community Statistical Areas rather than Baltimore's 256 neighborhood statistical areas: the neighborhood set is finer than the 1 km radius this index measures over, so neighboring pages would describe almost the same circle. CSAs group those neighborhoods on census-tract lines — the same geography the city uses for its own indicator reporting — so each page covers a distinct part of the city. Time-of-day charts use BPD incident timestamps, severity-weighted. Pages regenerate as new data is published.
