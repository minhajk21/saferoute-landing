Is San Francisco safe at night?

46% of San Francisco's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 19% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 38% to 57% depending on which neighborhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether San Francisco is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is San Francisco safe to walk at night?

46% of San Francisco's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 19% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighborhoods than it does between cities, from 38% to 57% here. The useful question is which neighborhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighborhood in San Francisco has the most reported crime at night?

Tenderloin, with roughly 4,423 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 44% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighborhood changes most after dark?

Marina is the most night-concentrated in San Francisco: 57% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a San Francisco median of 47%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighborhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

How does SafeRoute use this at night?

The app scores each walking route against the same SFPD data, and can weight it toward the time you are actually walking rather than a flat all-day average. It also shows how much of each route runs on lit streets, and lets you share a walk.

Methodology

Every incident reported to the SFPD carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighborhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighborhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each incident reported to the SFPD (via DataSF) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center, and normalize against citywide crime rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are DataSF's 41 Analysis Neighborhoods, the same unit SFPD tags each report with. Because San Francisco's dense downtown far outweighs its residential west side, the index is calibrated to the citywide median so a typical neighborhood reads mid-scale. Time-of-day charts use SFPD incident timestamps, severity-weighted. Pages regenerate as new data is published.
