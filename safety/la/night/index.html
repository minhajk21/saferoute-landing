Is Los Angeles safe at night?

48% of Los Angeles's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 18% of them fall between midnight and 6am. That share swings from 38% to 58% depending on which neighborhood you are in — which is the whole point: the question worth asking is not whether Los Angeles is safe after dark, but where and by which route.

Common questions

Is Los Angeles safe to walk at night?

48% of Los Angeles's reported incidents happen between 6pm and 6am, and 18% fall in the small hours between midnight and 6am. That is a description of when incidents are reported, not a verdict on the city: the figure varies far more between neighborhoods than it does between cities, from 38% to 58% here. The useful question is which neighborhood and which route, not whether a whole city is safe.

Which neighborhood in Los Angeles has the most reported crime at night?

Downtown Los Angeles, with roughly 5,352 reported incidents between 6pm and 6am — 48% of its total. Somewhere busy usually tops this list because more people are out, so read it alongside the daytime picture on its own page rather than on its own.

Which neighborhood changes most after dark?

Echo Park is the most night-concentrated in Los Angeles: 58% of what is reported there happens between 6pm and 6am, against a Los Angeles median of 48%. A high share does not mean a high total — a quiet neighborhood whose few incidents mostly happen at night will rank high here — which is why the table shows the count beside it.

Methodology

Every incident reported to the LAPD carries an occurrence time, which is bucketed into daytime (6am–6pm), evening (6pm–midnight) and the small hours (midnight–6am), split weekday against weekend, and weighted by severity so violence counts for more than shoplifting. A neighborhood's night share is the weighted proportion falling between 6pm and 6am; its night count is that share applied to its total. The city-wide chart weights each neighborhood by its own volume, so a busy centre is not averaged against a quiet suburb as though they were the same size.

Each incident reported to the LAPD (via LA Open Data) is weighted by severity — violence counts for more than shoplifting. For every neighborhood we sum weighted incidents within 1 km of its center, and normalize against citywide crime rates onto a 0–100 index, higher = safer. Boundaries are the City of LA's 99 Neighborhood Councils; neighboring cities with their own police (Santa Monica, Beverly Hills, Culver City and others) are outside LAPD's data and are not scored. Where a council spans large parkland, the map centers on its populated core rather than its geometric middle. Time-of-day charts use LAPD incident timestamps, severity-weighted. Locations are published to the nearest hundred block for privacy. Pages regenerate as new data is published.
